# The 20 composition rules, adapted for video frames

The rule list follows the deck "20 rules of photo composition"
(https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/20-rules-of-photo-composition/248755645,
fetched 2026-10-03). The explanations, video adaptations, and tests are written
for this skill.

Rules conflict on purpose (fill the frame vs negative space, thirds vs centered).
Each scene picks the rules that serve its purpose and states any rule it breaks.

| # | Rule | Video adaptation | Pass test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rule of thirds** | Focal point on a thirds line or power point. The default for product and speaker shots | Focal point within 3% of a thirds line |
| 2 | **Centered composition and symmetry** | Logo reveals, single statements, end cards. Symmetry reads as still and formal | Mirrored weight within ~10%. Center chosen on purpose, not by default |
| 3 | **Foreground interest and depth** | A blurred foreground prop or UI chip in front of the subject adds depth. Pair it with parallax | At least two depth layers moving at different speeds when the scene moves |
| 4 | **Frame within the frame** | Device bezels, cards, masks, and window chrome enclose the subject | One frame level. The inner frame does not touch the outer frame edge |
| 5 | **Leading lines** | UI edges, arrows, connector lines, and gradient directions point at the focal point | Every strong line ends at or points to the focal point, not off-frame |
| 6 | **Diagonals and triangles** | Tilted devices and angled cards add energy. Three elements arranged in a triangle feel stable | Diagonals run toward the focal point. Triangle groups have a clear apex |
| 7 | **Patterns and textures** | Repeated cards, grids of logos, and background textures give rhythm. Break the pattern once to mark the focal item | The focal item breaks the pattern by color, scale, or position |
| 8 | **Rule of odds** | Show 3 or 5 items. Odd groups give the eye a center item to rest on | Item count is odd, or even on purpose for a comparison |
| 9 | **Fill the frame** | Close crops on a UI detail or product surface for emphasis beats | The subject takes over ~70% of the frame. No unrelated element enters |
| 10 | **Leave negative space** | Empty area around the subject signals premium and gives text and motion room | Meets the white-space budget in SKILL.md section 3 |
| 11 | **Simplicity and minimalism** | One idea per hold frame. Cut decoration that does not support the beat | Removing any element would lose meaning |
| 12 | **Isolate the subject** | Separate the subject with blur, a darkened background, a spotlight gradient, or a solid field | The squint check finds the subject first |
| 13 | **Change your point of view** | Vary the virtual camera: top-down, low angle, 3D tilt on a device. Avoid every scene being flat front-on | Consecutive scenes do not repeat the same angle and scale |
| 14 | **Look for particular color** | One accent color marks the focal point. Everything else stays neutral | The accent color appears on the focal element and nowhere competing |
| 15 | **Rule of space** | Lead room. Open space goes in the direction the subject faces, points, or moves | The space ahead of the subject is wider than the space behind it |
| 16 | **Left to right rule** | Progress, timelines, and entering content flow left to right for LTR viewers. Reverse only to mean back or against | Direction matches meaning. Flip for RTL scripts |
| 17 | **Balance elements in the scene** | Offset a heavy element with a smaller, higher contrast one or with empty space on the opposite side | The frame does not feel like it tips toward one side when squinted |
| 18 | **Juxtaposition** | Place contrasting items side by side (before and after, old and new, problem and fix) to tell a story in one frame | The two items share size and alignment so only the difference reads |
| 19 | **Golden triangles** | Diagonal compositions place subjects on the triangle anchors (see grids-and-ratios.md) | Subject within 3% of a triangle anchor. Main lines follow the diagonal |
| 20 | **Golden ratio** | Phi grid at 0.382 and 0.618, golden spiral for one hero with a detail, 61.8/38.2 splits | Focal point within 3% of a phi line, or the spiral eye on the detail |
