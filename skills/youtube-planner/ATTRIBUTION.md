# Attribution

`luckiest-youtube-planner` is an original Luckiest skill. Its method is written in
our own words. No text or code was copied from the sources below.

- The stage chain (a video fails if any one stage fails), the floor and ceiling
  of a topic, bundling demand to raise the ceiling, cross-niche packaging
  patterns and the overfitting warning, the two thumbnail jobs, the four-P hook
  (promise, proof, plan, persona), outlines built from acceptance criteria for
  the title's promise, satisfaction over retention, and reading metrics as places
  to look: from Jake Trinder's public video "Give Me 15 Minutes and I'll Make You
  Disgustingly Good at YouTube" (YouTube, 2026-10-14, video id Dx-c19qXdxM).
- The story loop in the body stage (stakes, open question, turn, reopen, with
  loops chained through the video): from Kallaway Marketing's public Facebook
  reel on storytelling loops
  (https://www.facebook.com/reel/4034613460177150, viewed 2026-10-16).
- Ranking a video by its views over its own channel's median (skipping channels
  with too few videos), linting the title and thumbnail as one pairing, the
  query test, reading a retention export as hook leak, cliffs, and slide, the
  opening-line rules, on-screen notes per beat, chapter rules and short section
  names, Short candidates with new first lines, comment triage, an audit that
  ends in one fix, and a voice file: ideas from Jakeschincariol/youtube-agent-skill
  (MIT, https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/youtube-agent-skill, reviewed at
  commit a2feb21). `pairing.py`, `retention.py`, and the `--channel` mode are
  our own code.
- Comments as a satisfaction readout: an idea from
  ScrapeCreators/social-media-research-skills (MIT,
  https://github.com/ScrapeCreators/social-media-research-skills).

Search data comes from yt-dlp (Unlicense, https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp),
called as an external tool. It is not bundled.
